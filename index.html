Hi there, and welcome to my website!

I’m Mathieu Bourret Soto. I hold a PhD in Economics from the Bordeaux School of Economics (University of Bordeaux), where I defended my thesis on 13 November 2026. My doctoral research examines the political economy of conflict and peacebuilding, using quasi-experimental and microeconometric methods to analyse the causes and consequences of violence in Latin America. I’m also interested in climate change adaptation, using a more interdisciplinary lens. From March to July 2025, I was a visiting scholar at NOVAFRICA (Nova SBE).

I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market. Civic organisation is usually seen as a shield against violence; in my job market paper, I show that in Colombia it can also expose, as the expansion of grassroots civic institutions raises targeted killings of social leaders where armed groups hold territory, while remaining neutral or protective elsewhere.

Research

Job Market Paper

  • Civic Expansion and Targeted Violence under Armed Governance: Evidence from Colombia. paper
    Abstract

    Do grassroots civic institutions protect local leaders from violence, or expose them? Using Colombia’s Juntas de Acción Comunal (JACs) and a novel 65-year administrative panel, I exploit within-municipality variation in annual JAC registrations in a continuous-treatment difference-in-differences design. On the full panel, civic expansion raises subsequent targeted killings of social leaders: the average causal effect is positive and significant, on the order of one additional killing for every ninety new JACs. This effect is concentrated in conflict municipalities and is neutral or protective where armed groups are absent; it is strongest where armed governance is durable, a descriptive gradient identified under stronger assumptions than the baseline. A second finding bounds the threat: killings do not reduce subsequent JAC registrations at any horizon, so targeting does not suppress the formation of new boards, though registration is an imperfect proxy for whether they keep functioning. A leader is targeted for their role in the network, not as an individual, so collective protection is the relevant policy lever.

Publications

  • “This is our Legacy”: Youth Exposure to Repression and Trust in Institutions, Journal of Institutional Economics, 22:e47, 2026. paper
    Abstract

    This paper examines how youth exposure to political repression is associated with long-term trust in public institutions in Chile, drawing on the impressionable years hypothesis. I exploit variation in proximity to pre-1970 military bases as a proxy for local repression intensity. Individuals who were in their critical period (ages 17–25) at the 1973 coup exhibit significantly lower trust in the Carabineros (national police) decades later, with similar effects for those aged 10–16. This effect is a relative one: the Carabineros remain the most trusted institution across all cohorts, with exposure creating a persistent trust gap rather than absolute mistrust. Trust in representative institutions (government, presidency) is not significantly affected, indicating institution-specific legacies. Migration, university attendance, and residence in the capital do not account for the results. Early-life exposure to repression is associated with a durable, institution-specific deficit in political trust, underscoring the long shadow of political violence on democratic consolidation.

  • Understanding Individual Adaptation to Arboviral Diseases Risk: A Systematic Review of Behavioural Determinants of Prevention and Meta-Analysis for non-tropical countries, with Elisa Delor and Marlène Guillon. International Journal of Infectious Diseases, 170(108845), 2026. paper
    Abstract

    The global spread of Aedes albopictus, a vector of dengue, chikungunya, and Zika, has caused rising autochthonous cases in non-tropical regions. This review synthesizes evidence on psychological and social determinants of individual preventive behaviors in non-endemic settings. Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, PubMed and Web of Science were searched for peer-reviewed quantitative studies published up to January 6, 2026. Eligible studies reported associations between individual-level psychosocial factors (e.g., risk perception) and preventive behaviors in adults. Effect sizes from multivariate analyses were converted to correlation coefficients (r) and pooled using random-effects meta-analysis. Moderator analyses examined zone type (subtropical vs temperate) and world region (Asia-Pacific, Europe, United States). Of 8531 unique records screened, 226 studies were included, with 62 in non-tropical settings. Thirteen variables across 39 studies met criteria for meta-analysis. Preventive behaviors were strongly influenced by perceived barriers, perception scores, risk perception, perceived severity, and knowledge about MBDs. The effect of knowledge was stronger in subtropical countries and Asia-Pacific countries compared to temperate countries and the United States. Tailored communication strategies in non-endemic regions are essential to support individual adaptation as Aedes albopictus expands. Campaigns should enhance public knowledge of MBDs as well as awareness of individual and collective health risks and highlight simple, effective preventive measures.

  • Prevalence and factors associated with the adoption of heat-adaptive behaviors among residents of a French southern region, with Marlène Guillon. Journal of Environmental Psychology, 109(102883), 2026. paper
    Abstract

    Heatwaves are becoming more frequent and intense, making it essential to examine individual protective strategies against their negative health effects. We conducted a survey (N = 1,515) in summer 2022 to investigate the prevalence of eight heat-adaptive behaviors (HABs) and the factors associated with their adoption. The population implemented the eight HABs heterogeneously, with closing shutters, blinds, or curtains and going to a cooler or air-conditioned place being the most and least adopted HABs, respectively. Using the Protective Action Decision Model (PADM), we found that greater perceived efficacy and descriptive norms were associated with higher adoption of HABs, as measured by an adaptation index, while greater perceived barriers led to lower adaptation. Conversely, demographic characteristics and self-reported health did not seem to be important predictors of adaptation. Our disaggregated analysis indicated that sociodemographic variables, such as gender or income, and threat perception variables exerted a heterogeneous impact on the eight HABs. Our study identified key drivers of HAB adoption that could help health authorities shape their communication campaigns to improve the population's adaptation to heatwaves.

  • What drives adaptive behaviours during heatwaves? A systematic review with a meta-analysis, with Marlène Guillon. Climate Policy, 24(8), 2024. paper
    Abstract

    Global warming will lead to more frequent and intense heatwaves across all regions of the world in the coming decades. Adaptation at the individual level plays a key role in helping populations cope with these extreme events. This study undertakes a systematic review of the academic literature on the determinants of heat-adaptive behaviours (HABs) following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A Boolean algorithm was used on two online databases (PubMed and Web of Science Core Collection) to retrieve 73 relevant, quantitative, peer-reviewed papers. All included studies focus on high-income economies (N = 48) or middle-income economies (MIEs, N = 25). A meta-analysis was performed on 34 studies using a random-effects model, with an emphasis on heat-related perceptions (perceived efficacy, risk and threat) and heat-related knowledge (HRK), as these variables are the most likely levers of behavioural change. Perceived efficacy, HRK, perceived threat and gender (i.e. being female) are positively associated with the adoption of HABs. A moderation analysis reveals heterogeneity in the effect of these variables by country income group, with low HRK and perceived threat constituting stronger barriers to HABs in MIEs. This study also highlights various gaps in the current literature, regarding the populations studied, the statistical methods used, and the lack of standardization of HABs and perception concepts.
